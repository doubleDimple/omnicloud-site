Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 24, 2026

This policy describes how the OmniCloud app for iPhone and iPad (“OmniCloud”, “the app”, “we”) handles information. OmniCloud connects your device to the cloud accounts and servers you choose. App data is stored locally, with optional synchronization to your own iCloud and server-side storage for the services you configure.

Summary

  • You do not need an OmniCloud account. The app has no developer-operated backend for your cloud data or credentials.
  • The app connects directly to Oracle Cloud, Hetzner Cloud, Google Cloud, Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, netcup and OVHcloud. Cloudflare is a separate workspace, and SSH and SFTP connect to servers you provide.
  • Cloud and SSH credentials are protected by the device Keychain. If you enable iCloud sync, eligible account and SSH credentials are also synchronized to your private iCloud database. Sync is off by default.
  • Apple handles subscriptions and optional iCloud sync. Currency conversion uses the European Central Bank’s public reference rates. There are no advertising or behavioral analytics SDKs in the app.

Data accessed through Google APIs

When you connect Google Cloud with Sign in with Google, OmniCloud requests these permissions:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/cloud-platform: to read and manage Google Cloud resources in the projects you choose.
  • openid and email: to show which Google account is connected.

When you connect a project and use its features, the app:

  • Lists your Google Cloud projects so that you can choose one.
  • In the project you choose:
    • Enables the Compute Engine, IAM, Cloud Resource Manager, Cloud Billing (public price catalog), Cloud Storage and Cloud Logging APIs.
    • Creates or reuses a service account named omnicloud-ios.
    • Grants it Compute Admin (roles/compute.admin), Service Account User (roles/iam.serviceAccountUser), Storage Admin (roles/storage.admin) and Logs Viewer (roles/logging.viewer).
    • Creates a key for it.
    If IAM changes or key creation are not allowed, the app can retain your Google refresh token instead. You can also connect by importing your own service account JSON key.
  • Reads, creates, starts, stops, resizes and deletes Compute Engine instances, and reads and changes VPC firewall rules and subnets.
  • Lists, creates and deletes Cloud Storage buckets; browses, uploads, downloads and deletes objects; and creates temporary read links where the connected credential supports signing.
  • Reads project quotas and audit-log entries that your permissions allow.
  • Reads Google’s public Compute Engine price catalog. This does not grant access to your billing account’s invoices or payment information.

Google data includes account identity, project and resource identifiers, instance and network details, storage metadata and object contents you choose to transfer, and accessible audit-log entries. The app uses this data to provide the cloud-management features you choose. Resource caches and request logs stay on the device. Connected account records and credentials, including a Google key or retained refresh token, can be synchronized through Apple’s private iCloud database only when you enable iCloud sync. Files you download or export are saved where you choose; a read link you share gives its recipient access until it expires.

We do not receive your Google cloud data through an OmniCloud backend, sell it, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI or machine-learning models.

OmniCloud’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Cloud providers and Cloudflare

Oracle Cloud, Hetzner Cloud, Google Cloud, AWS, Microsoft Azure, netcup and OVHcloud connections use each provider’s official sign-in flow or the credentials you supply, as supported by that provider. The app uses the resulting credentials to call its official APIs directly. Available resource-management actions differ between providers.

The separate Cloudflare workspace lets you sign in on Cloudflare’s official dashboard to create an API token, or supply a token yourself. The token is used for the DNS, security, cache, tunnel and other supported account tools you choose. Its connection records and token remain on this device and are not included in the app’s iCloud sync. Provider server-purchasing credentials and order records are also kept separately on this device. Each provider processes your requests under its own privacy policy.

Optional iCloud sync

iCloud sync is off by default and is available with a paid membership. When you turn it on, the app synchronizes cloud-account records and credentials, supported provider sign-in tokens, SSH host profiles and credentials, trusted host keys, saved command snippets and selected account preferences to your own private CloudKit database. The app writes the record contents and credentials into CloudKit’s encrypted fields.

Resource caches, request logs, Cloudflare workspace connections, separate server-purchasing credentials and order records are not included. Turning sync off stops synchronization on this device but keeps the existing iCloud copy. The app provides a separate action to delete its iCloud data without deleting this device’s local data.

Your servers and certificates

SSH, SFTP, terminal commands and server-management tools communicate directly with the servers you configure. Websites & Certificates runs Nginx installation, reverse-proxy configuration and certificate tasks on a supported server you provide. The server retains its Nginx configuration, certificate private keys, certificate contact email, dedicated DNS-validation token, task records and renewal configuration. The app keeps task identifiers locally so you can reconnect and check progress.

For installation and certificate issuance, that server contacts its system package repositories, the official acme.sh repository, Let’s Encrypt and Cloudflare. The dedicated, zone-scoped Cloudflare token is sent to your server for DNS validation and automatic renewal; it is separate from the app’s Cloudflare workspace token and is not saved as an app credential. Let’s Encrypt receives the requested domain names and contact email. Issued certificates and their domain names are public through Certificate Transparency logs.

Local request logs

The request log records time, request method, destination host, URL path, API action where available, response status, duration and whether an API request or SSH connection failed. Paths and addresses can include resource identifiers. Request headers, bodies and URL query strings are omitted, and SSH command text is not recorded. The log stays on this device, is excluded from backups and iCloud sync, and is not sent to us.

When the log is opened or written, the app removes entries older than 30 days and keeps at most 50,000 entries. You can also delete individual entries or clear the log in the app.

Sharing

The app sends information needed for the features you choose to the connected cloud provider, Cloudflare or your server. Optional iCloud sync uses your Apple account; subscription purchase and entitlement verification use Apple’s App Store services. The app reads public European Central Bank exchange rates without including your cloud-account or spending data in those requests. Certificate-related services are described above. We do not sell your data.

Retention and deletion

  • Data & Privacy can clear cached resource data or remove this device’s connected accounts, SSH profiles, Cloudflare connections, associated credentials, local order and website-task records, and request logs.
  • Removing local data turns iCloud sync off first and keeps the iCloud copy. Delete that copy separately using the app’s iCloud data-deletion action.
  • Deleting the app removes its local database and cache. iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted; use the app’s local data-removal action before uninstalling to remove its stored credentials. Uninstalling does not revoke provider access, cancel an Apple subscription or delete your iCloud copy.

Resources the app created in your cloud accounts, such as the omnicloud-ios service account and servers, belong to you and remain until you delete them. Removing app data also leaves Nginx, certificate files, server-side DNS credentials, task history and renewal schedules on your server. Manage those on the server, and revoke provider credentials at the provider when you no longer need them.

Revoking access

Google Cloud

  • Remove OmniCloud’s access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • In the Google Cloud console, under IAM & Admin › Service accounts, you can delete the omnicloud-ios service account or its keys.
  • Removing Google sign-in access does not delete a separately created service account key. Remove the local connection and any optional iCloud copy separately if you also want to erase the app’s saved records.

Other providers and servers

Use your provider’s account or security settings to revoke authorizations, API keys or tokens used by OmniCloud. For Cloudflare, the workspace token and any separate token stored on your server for certificate renewal must be managed separately. To revoke SSH access, remove the authorized public key or change the relevant password on your server. Removing a connection from the app or turning off iCloud sync does not itself revoke access at the provider or server.

Security

Credentials on the device are protected by the system Keychain; optional synchronization uses encrypted CloudKit record fields. Provider API requests use HTTPS, while SSH and SFTP use encrypted SSH connections directly to your server. Websites and upstream services you configure may use HTTP or HTTPS according to your settings. You can also protect access to the app with Face ID or your device passcode.

Children

OmniCloud is a tool for managing cloud infrastructure and is not directed to children under 13.
